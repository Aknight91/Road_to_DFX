
# ROAD TO DFX
### The Oregon Trail of PCB Design

*You have been assigned a new PCB design project. The schedule is aggressive. The requirements are changing. Manufacturing has questions. Your ECAD software has stopped responding.*

**Good luck. You're going to need it.**

---

## What is Road to DFX?

Road to DFX is an Oregon Trail-inspired game about the joys, challenges, and occasional absurdities of getting a PCB design ready for manufacturing.

Instead of guiding a wagon train across the American frontier, you're leading an engineering team through the treacherous landscape of PCB design.

Your mission: deliver a design that meets its Design for Excellence (DFX) goals before the schedule runs out, the engineering hours disappear, or your sanity reaches its breaking point.

Because getting a PCB designed is one thing.

Getting it manufactured successfully? That's another adventure entirely.

## Your Mission

Navigate a series of engineering decisions and survive the consequences.

You'll need to balance four critical areas:

| Attribute | What it means |
|---|---|
| **DFA** | Design for Assembly |
| **DFM** | Design for Manufacturing |
| **DFT** | Design for Test |
| **Sanity** | Your team's ability to survive the project |

Every decision has consequences. Sometimes the right engineering solution costs time. Sometimes the quick fix creates a bigger problem later.

And sometimes you just have to ask yourself:

*Is this really worth another ECO?*

## Features and Mechanics

The prototype is being developed around several core systems:

- **Engineering Events:** Respond to late design changes, manufacturing feedback, DRC violations, and other engineering surprises.
- **Meaningful Decisions:** Each choice can affect engineering resources, schedule, DFX performance, and team sanity.
- **Resource Management:** Engineering hours, remaining days, and manufacturing risk all matter.
- **DFX Progression:** Work toward meeting the project's DFA, DFM, and DFT goals.
- **Sanity Crisis:** Hitting zero doesn't mean the project is immediately over. It means the situation has become considerably more interesting. You have a limited number of decisions to pull off a miracle.
- **Campaign Outcomes:** Your decisions determine whether the design makes it to release or becomes another engineering cautionary tale.

More systems and events will be added as development continues.

## Choose Your Difficulty

Not every project starts out equally painful.

The planned difficulty levels are:

- **Smooth Sailing** — Surely this one will be straightforward.
- **Business as Usual** — A normal engineering project. Nothing to worry about.
- **Death March** — The schedule was probably approved by someone who doesn't do the work.
- **COW Mode (Can of Worms)** — You don't know what's wrong with the design. You just know something is wrong.

*Difficulty settings are planned features and are still under development.*

## Development

Road to DFX is being built from scratch using **GameMaker**.

The project is an evolving prototype, with development focused on implementing and testing the underlying game mechanics before expanding the interface and presentation.

The visual direction is inspired by classic 1990s Oregon Trail-era PC games, with a PCB engineering twist.

The long-term goal is a mouse-driven, point-and-click experience.

## Current Status

<em>Under active development.</em>

The current prototype includes:
- A working campaign controller.
- Engineering event and decision systems.
- Resource and schedule tracking.
- DRC and manufacturing-risk mechanics.
- DFA, DFM, DFT, and Sanity status tracking.
- Initial sanity-crisis and campaign outcome logic.

Additional events, difficulty balancing, software reliability consequences, and further gameplay systems are planned.

## Why Build This?

PCB designers and engineers know the drill.

A simple design change becomes a routing exercise. A routing exercise becomes a DRC cleanup. A DRC cleanup becomes a manufacturing review. A manufacturing review becomes an ECO.

And somewhere along the way, the software crashes.

Road to DFX is a humorous tribute to the engineering process, the people who keep projects moving, and the countless decisions that stand between a schematic and a manufacturable product.

It's a game, but the inspiration is painfully real.
